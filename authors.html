The writers

Meet the Authors

The pens behind the library — from Jane Austen's drawing rooms to Manto's Partition streets, from Ghalib's Delhi to Doyle's Baker Street. Short introductions, with links to read each writer's work free.

1775 – 1817 · England

Jane Austen

The sharpest observer of human character in English fiction. Her novels — written with wit that still feels modern — dissect pride, prejudice, money, and marriage in Regency England. She published anonymously in her lifetime; today she is among the most beloved novelists who ever lived.

On AST Novels: Pride and Prejudice
1797 – 1851 · England

Mary Shelley

At eighteen she wrote Frankenstein — and invented science fiction. Born to radical thinkers, widowed young, she poured grief and genius into a tale of creation and responsibility that has haunted readers for two centuries.

On AST Novels: Frankenstein
1847 – 1912 · Ireland

Bram Stoker

A Dublin theatre manager who wrote the definitive vampire novel. Dracula (1897), told through letters and diaries, turned folklore into a modern myth — the Count has never left our imagination since.

On AST Novels: Dracula
1832 – 1898 · England

Lewis Carroll

Charles Lutwidge Dodgson — Oxford mathematician, photographer, and dreamer — wrote Alice's Adventures in Wonderland for a real child on a boat trip. Beneath the nonsense lies some of the cleverest logic ever smuggled into a children's book.

On AST Novels: Alice's Adventures in Wonderland
1859 – 1930 · Scotland

Arthur Conan Doyle

A doctor who created the world's most famous detective. Sherlock Holmes — with his violin, his cocaine, and his impossible deductions — redefined crime fiction. Doyle eventually tired of Holmes, but readers never did.

On AST Novels: The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes
1819 – 1891 · America

Herman Melville

A sailor who wrote the great American novel. Moby Dick — Captain Ahab's obsessive hunt for the white whale — was ignored in his lifetime and is now considered one of literature's towering achievements.

On AST Novels: Moby Dick
1850 – 1894 · Scotland

Robert Louis Stevenson

The sickly Edinburgh boy who became the master of adventure. Treasure Island gave the world Long John Silver, treasure maps marked with an X, and the pirate voice we all imitate.

On AST Novels: Treasure Island
1809 – 1849 · America

Edgar Allan Poe

The father of the detective story and the master of American dread. His poems and tales — musical, dark, precise — mapped the geography of guilt and madness like no one before him.

On AST Novels: The Tell-Tale Heart
1862 – 1910 · America

O. Henry

William Sydney Porter — bank clerk, convict, then the most popular short-story writer in America. His twist endings became legend; The Gift of the Magi remains the most tender story ever written about being poor at Christmas.

On AST Novels: The Gift of the Magi

Author FAQs

Which authors can I read on AST Novels?
The library includes Jane Austen, Mary Shelley, Bram Stoker, Lewis Carroll, Arthur Conan Doyle, Herman Melville, Robert Louis Stevenson, Edgar Allan Poe, O. Henry, Saadat Hasan Manto, Allama Iqbal, and Mirza Ghalib — all free to read.

Who is Saadat Hasan Manto?
Saadat Hasan Manto (1912–1955) is the most celebrated short-story writer in Urdu literature, famous for brutally honest stories about Partition such as Toba Tek Singh, Khol Do, and Thanda Gosht — all available in original Urdu on AST Novels.

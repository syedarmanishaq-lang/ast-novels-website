The case for one author's shelf

Why Six Novels From One Pen?

Because they were written by one person who refused to rush. Arman Ishaq began his first novel at 15 and finished it at 17 — then kept writing, in Urdu and in English, until the shelf held six books. Every chapter here was read many times over before it was published, and nothing here is filler.

These books repay the effort they ask. Amanat-e-Noor is a story of a trust that must be kept, not given. Jala Hua Khat hides a truth inside a burnt letter. Har Jahan asks thirteen questions — about love, family, time, money, and the hereafter — and answers each one in its own world.

And the language itself is a reward. The Urdu novels are written in the original Urdu script, set in Nastaliq type; the English novels are written in English from the start. These books are not homework; they are page-turners, written to be finished in one more chapter.
